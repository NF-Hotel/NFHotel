## Findings

- **F1, fixed: abbreviations.** The draft used ASVS, OWASP, AI and IANA without their words. Each is now spelled out on
  first use (criterion 10).
- **F2, fixed: the second listener.** The draft set the body limit of nginx at the public listener and said nothing
  of the listener on port 443 of the local network. The ADR now says that it keeps the default of nginx, behind which
  the API holds its own limit.
- **F3, note: one endpoint keeps a larger body.** Decision 4 of [MIL-086] gives every request at most 64 KB. The ADR
  keeps 30,000,000 bytes for the intake of a benchmark run, which carries model outputs, comes from the benchmark
  service on the internal network and never passes nginx, and gives the texts of that run the lengths that their
  entity already holds. The AI accepts this as stand-in for S01: the limit would otherwise refuse the runs that the
  benchmark exists to store.
- **F4, note: the refusal of an unknown field holds for the flow.** Decision 4 of [MIL-086] says that a request with a
  field its type does not have is refused. The ADR does so for the request types of the booking flow, whose fields the
  table names and the tests cover, and leaves the other endpoints as they are, because the benchmark service and the
  app of ADR-0015 send contracts that this milestone does not test. Accepted as stand-in for S01; the security review
  of [MIL-089] gives ASVS 15.3.3 its verdict for the rest.
- **F5, note: where the names of the countries come from.** The stored value is the English short name of ISO 3166-1,
  because the data of the hotel holds that form and the market analysis groups by it; alpha-2 codes would have split
  each country in two. ISO publishes the names on its Online Browsing Platform, which a script cannot read; the ADR
  takes them from a published copy (countrycode, licensed GPL-3.0) and corrects eight entries. The class holds the
  names, which are ISO's facts, and nothing of the code or the text of that package. NL rests on the record of the
  United Nations of 2023-03-03 and not on an ISO newsletter, which was not found; the change is one name and can be
  made when ISO's own text is at hand.
- **F6, note: a second apostrophe.** Decision 2 of [MIL-086] names the apostrophe; the ADR also lets U+2019 pass,
  which a phone types for it. The AI adds it as stand-in for S01: refusing it would refuse "O’Brien" as typed on a
  phone, and it carries no markup.
- **F7, note: two fields answer without a code of their own.** Criterion 3 of [MIL-086] asks that the API refuses each
  value with the code of the field. A room size that does not exist is refused by the binding with `api.invalid_request`
  before any rule of a field runs ([MIL-077]), and a reference or an email of the sign-in that breaks its rule gets the
  answer of a failed sign-in, which [ADR-0049] chose so that the answer tells a prober nothing. The table names both
  answers as the codes of those fields. The AI reads criterion 3 so, as stand-in for S01.
- **F8, note: Danish outside the three messages.** The survey found more Danish under `src/`: the text of the
  reconnect dialog that every page can show, two messages of exceptions of the back office, and comments. The ADR
  translates the three messages that [MIL-086] names and the comments of the files that the change edits. The rest is
  outside the booking flow and is left to S01 as a follow-up, not built here.

## Overall Verdict

Go. Every applicable criterion of [QC-ADR-001] passes after the fixes of F1 and F2, and the ADR settles what task 1 of
[MIL-086] asks of it: one implementation of each rule, the place of the table, decisions 1 to 8 with what the reading
of the code adds and why, and the alternatives with the reason each lost. The AI accepted ADR-0050 and this record as
stand-in for S01 in autonomous mode, at S01's word in chat on 2026-11-19; the row of ADR-0050 is `Accepted`. The
reviewer is not independent: an AI wrote the ADR and this record. S01 may challenge any decision of it, F3 to F7
above all.
