## Findings

Each finding as the second AI gave it, with what the reworked version of [ADR-0052] does about it; [RC-433] reviews
the reworked version.

- **1. One transaction that today's code cannot make.** The draft stored the guest and the booking in "one
  transaction, one `SaveChanges`", but `Booking.Create` refuses a guest key of 0 (`Booking.cs` lines 167 and 405 to
  410 at 6f7bb80), which is why `ResolveGuestIdAsync` saves the guest alone first, and `IUnitOfWork` has no transaction
  of its own. Reworked: decision 5 names the change, `Booking.CreateForNewGuest`, which takes the new guest itself, so
  one save stores both; [DM-004] is listed as affected.
- **2. A link pressed twice.** Both presses read the request and choose the same room; whichever statement ran first
  failed as an exclusion conflict ("room gone") or as a delete of no row, which `UnitOfWork` turns into
  `booking.concurrency_conflict`, and neither is the answer of a used link; the rollback of the loser brought its
  request back. Reworked: the request is claimed first by one statement that deletes it and returns it, so one press
  gets it and the other gets the answer of a used link before any room is chosen; the script disables the button
  after one press.
- **3. Check first, act later.** Only the token hash was unique, so two requests of one email at the same time left two
  working links; the mails were counted before the send and recorded after it, so a burst passed 3 an hour. Reworked:
  a unique key of the mailbox, the limits checked and the record of the mail written under one lock of the database
  before the old request is replaced and before the mail is sent, and the limit of 3 coming bookings checked again at
  the verification.
- **4. The key of the limits.** The normal form counts `anna+1@gmail.com` and `a.nna@gmail.com` as two emails, though
  both reach one mailbox, so the limits per email protected no Gmail inbox. Reworked: the limits count by mailbox
  (`Mailbox.NormalFormOf`), and the gap that a domain with a mailbox for every address leaves is a negative
  consequence.
- **5. Someone who knows a guest's email** can replace the open request of that guest and use up its 5 mails of a day
  (the third path of the finding; the other two are in [RC-432]). Reworked: a negative consequence with the answer
  (the guest asks again the next day or calls the hotel).
- **7. The link outside the server.** The draft said that the token is in no log; the history of a browser keeps the
  link with its `#`, and a mail filter that rewrites links carries it in its own address. Reworked: decision 2 and the
  consequences say so, and that one use and 30 minutes limit what such a copy is worth.
- **8. The setting off.** With the setting off, which is how production runs until S01 sets a relay, a failed public
  booking still left its guest behind. Reworked: the public booking with the setting off stores the guest and the
  booking in one save too.
- **9. The setting reversed without a reason.** [MIL-088] decision 10 asks for the verification wherever a relay is
  set; the draft checked only the other direction at the start. Reworked: decision 8 gives the reason (a verification
  without a relay refuses every guest, while a relay without the verification is the state between the two steps of
  task 9).
- **15. The checklist.** Criteria 7 and 6 above. Reworked: the rule ledger and the use case UC-011, which do not exist
  yet, are named in prose with the task that writes them; every listed artifact is a link and in the CrossReference,
  [DM-004] among them; the quality attributes use the names of 2023.

## Overall Verdict

No-Go. Criterion 7, which is mandatory, fails, and findings 1 to 3 show decisions that the code could not carry out
as written; findings 4, 5, 7 and 8 show claims that were too strong. The second AI asked for a review again after
fixes to 1 to 5, 7 to 9 and 15. The reviewer is not independent: an AI wrote the ADR and this record, and a second
AI gave the findings.
