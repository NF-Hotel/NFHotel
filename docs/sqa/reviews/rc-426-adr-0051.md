## Findings

- **F1, fixed: an abbreviation.** The draft used AI without its words in the Context. It is now spelled out at its
  first use.
- **F2, fixed: a source that was not read.** The draft gave the bound of the rate limiting middleware as read in a
  page of Microsoft that was not read for this record. The sentence now stands in option E as the reason the option
  lost, from how the partitioned limiter keeps its limiters.
- **F3, note: staff are counted by the search.** [MIL-087] decision 2 counts 60 searches a minute per address without
  naming the public site. The ADR counts the search of the back office too, because one endpoint serves both and
  staff search from other addresses than the guests; 60 a minute leaves a busy reception alone.
- **F4, note: the time to wait.** [MIL-087] decision 2 asks for one text and the time to wait. The ADR puts the time
  in `Retry-After` and lets the one text of the pages name no number, because the windows are a minute, an hour and a
  day and the pages do not read the header. A guest who meets the limit of the search waits a minute without being
  told so; the alternative, one text for each window, breaks the one text that the decision asks for.
- **F5, note: what the refusal per email tells.** The refusal per email has no `Retry-After`, and a stranger who
  types an email can learn that it has three coming public bookings, though not which or when. The same refusal
  answers the limits per address, but a stranger with a fresh address can tell them apart. Accepted as low: nothing of
  a booking is shown.
- **F6, note: the race of the limit per email.** The count per email and the insert are not one transaction. ASVS
  2.3.4 (a lock so that a limited thing is not given twice) is a requirement of [MIL-088], whose verification stores
  the guest and the booking in one transaction; the limits per address bound the race until then.
- **F7, note: the address in a circuit needs the stack to prove it.** Decision 4 takes the address into a circuit
  through the authentication state, the path that Microsoft documents and that the staff token takes, but a unit test
  cannot show that Blazor fills it from the connection of the circuit. Task 3 must show it through nginx in the test
  stack with an interactive page: a staff action in the back office, which runs in a circuit, must be audited under
  the address that nginx saw.
- **F8, note: bookings before the deploy.** They do not count per email, because nothing tells which of them came
  from the public site. The limit counts from the deploy on.

## Overall Verdict

Go. Every applicable criterion of [QC-ADR-001] passes after the fixes of F1 and F2, and the ADR settles what task 1 of
[MIL-087] asks of it: the seven decisions with their numbers, where each count lives and what a restart does to it,
how the address travels from nginx to the API and why the caller cannot write it, and the four alternatives that the
milestone names with the reason each lost, with four more. No number of [MIL-087] changes, so neither the milestone
nor success criterion 48 of [BC-001] changes. The AI accepted ADR-0051 and this record as stand-in for S01 in
autonomous mode, at S01's word in chat on 2026-11-19; the row of ADR-0051 is `Accepted`. The reviewer is not
independent: an AI wrote the ADR and this record. S01 may challenge any decision of it, F3 to F5 above all.
