with an empty cookie jar and then with the jar of the first; the names of the cookies are shown, their values never:

GET /booking, the same jar: X-Frame-Options: (none); cookies set: (none)
GET /book, empty jar: X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN; cookies set: .AspNetCore.Antiforgery.<suffix>
GET /book, the same jar: X-Frame-Options: (none); cookies set: (none)
GET /, empty jar: X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN; cookies set: .AspNetCore.Antiforgery.<suffix>
GET /, the same jar: X-Frame-Options: (none); cookies set: (none)
So the antiforgery of ASP.NET Core writes X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN on the answer that sets a new antiforgery
cookie, as it does by default; the application sets no such header of its own. Every answer carries frame-ancestors
'none', which a browser that knows CSP level 2 obeys, ignoring X-Frame-Options.
